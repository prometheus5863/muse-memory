# Sourdough

## Starter maintenance
I feed the starter 1:5:5 (starter:flour:water) twice a day when it lives on the counter. In the fridge it only needs a feed once a week. A healthy starter doubles within 4-6 hours at 24°C and smells mildly of yoghurt; a nail-polish smell means it is hungry and producing acetone-like by-products.

## Bulk fermentation
Bulk fermentation is where most loaves fail. I stop bulk when the dough has risen about 50%, has a domed edge, and shows bubbles on the sides of the container. At 24°C this takes around 5 hours with 20% starter. Cold kitchens in winter can stretch it to 8 hours.

## Hydration
My default loaf is 75% hydration with 10% whole wheat. Above 80% the dough becomes hard to shape without good strength from coil folds. Whole wheat absorbs more water, so increase hydration by about 2% for every 10% whole wheat.

## Scoring and baking
Bake in a preheated Dutch oven at 250°C for 20 minutes lid on, then 230°C for 20-25 minutes lid off. Score at a shallow 30-degree angle to get an ear. Steam from the lid is what lets the loaf expand before the crust sets.

## Lactic acid bacteria
The sourness comes from lactic acid bacteria in the starter, which produce lactic and acetic acid. Longer cold retards in the fridge favour acetic acid and a tangier loaf. The same bacteria families show up in other ferments like kimchi and yoghurt.
